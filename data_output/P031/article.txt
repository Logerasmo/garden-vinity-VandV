The effect of remnant CdSe layers on the performance of CdSeTe/CdTe
photovoltaic devices

A R T I C L E  I N F O                  A B S T R A C T

Keywords:                                     Thin film CdTe-based photovoltaic devices have achieved high efficiency above 22 %. The recent improvement in
CdSeTe/CdTe solar cell                                efficiency is due to Se alloying in the CdTe absorbers to form a CdSeTe/CdTe structure. The subsequent band gap
CdSe remnant                                    grading increases the short circuit current density. The Se can be introduced by depositing a precursor thin film
Grain boundary                                                     of either CdSe or a CdSeTe alloy and then diffusing the Se into the CdTe during the high temperature cadmiumParasitic absorption                                                   chloride activation process. Using CdSe is preferred because it is easier to control the Se concentration. However,
                                               during fabrication of the CdSeTe/CdTe devices, the CdSe thickness needs to be precisely controlled to prevent the
                                                   retention of a CdSe remnant layer after the activation treatment. Retention of a remnant CdSe layer causes a
                                               dramatic reduction in device efficiency. In this work, we show that the reduction in efficiency is caused by a
                                        number of factors. The remnant CdSe layer is n-type which moves the position of the p-n junction. Also, it is
                                              widely thought that the CdSe remnants are photo-inactive. In this work, we clarify that the individual CdSe
                                                   grains are actually highly photo-active. However, the grain sizes in the CdSe remnant and the adjacent CdSeTe
                                                    layer are very small resulting in a high grain boundary area. Although the grain boundaries are passivated with
                                                     chlorine, cathodoluminescence imaging and electrical measurements show that this is only partially effective.
                                                    Also, EQE measurements show that the remnant CdSe causes parasitic absorption. Overall, the remnant CdSe
                                                    layer causes a reduction in short circuit current density and device efficiency. The thickness of the CdSe precursor
                                                    layer and the cadmium chloride activation process conditions must be precisely optimised to ensure that all the
                                          CdSe is consumed and inter-diffused to form the CdSeTe alloy for highest efficiency devices.

                                                                            layer is preferred in manufacturing because the thin film composition is
                                                                             fixed and easily reproducible. However, although CdSe alloying signif­
                                                                              icantly enhances the device efficiency, its thickness needs to be precisely
                                                                           controlled to ensure the formation of the CdSeTe and avoid any

                                                                              of FTO/CdS/CdSe/CdTe. In this device configuration, the CdS layer is

                                                             The device structure used here is FTO/intrinsic SnO2/CdSe/CdTe. The
                                                                                   intrinsic SnO2 layer is the n-type emitter once the CdSe precursor is
                                                                     completely alloyed with the CdTe. It is often assumed that the intrinsic
                                                            SnO2 layer is highly resistive. This is a misconception because it actually
                                                                    has a carrier concentration in the order of 1018~1019 cm−3 [12,13].
                                                                               In a device with an intrinsic SnO2 emitter, it is widely thought that
                                                               any remnant CdSe  is  photo-inactive. Here we show  using  cath­
                                                                   odoluminescence on device cross-sections in transmission electron mi­
                                                                         croscopy, that the thin film CdSe is highly photo-active. The problem
                                                                    with the remnant CdSe is connected to its n-type electrical behaviour
                                                              and the small-grained nature of its hexagonal structure. With this in­
                                                                         formation, we are now able to more accurately model the device
                                                                           operation.

                                                                                 3.2. Electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD)

                                                                                      Fig. 3 shows an EBSD image of a cross-section of the CdSe/CdTe stack
                                                                    with a CdSe precursor thickness of 120 nm before the CdCl2 activation
                                                                         treatment. The CdTe grains show a columnar structure with a small
                                                                          grain size of ~500 nm in the lateral direction (Fig. 3a). The grain ori­
                                                                           entations of both the CdTe and the CdSe are poorly indexed in EBSD due
                                                                            to the presence of a high density of stacking faults in both layers. Fig. 3b
                                                               shows a phase map for the sample in which the cubic and hexagonal
                                                               components are coloured blue and yellow, respectively. Interestingly,
                                                                   hexagonal and cubic components are identified in both the as-deposited
                                                           CdTe and CdSe films. Additionally, the CdSe layer consists of remark­
                                                                       ably small grains (<100 nm).
                                                                               After the activation treatment, the EBSD analysis in Fig. 4a shows
                                                                             that the CdSe/CdTe stack formed with the 120 nm thick CdSe precursor
                                                                    has been completely converted to the cubic phase. The CdSe layer had,
                                                                          apparently, been completely consumed during the activation treatment
                                                              and alloyed with CdTe to form a CdSeTe alloy. The hexagonal compo­
                                                                     nent in the untreated device is no longer present due to the complete
                                                                 removal of stacking faults throughout the absorber [14]. In addition, the
                                                              CdSeTe and CdTe grains have recrystallised and grown larger during the
                                                                             activation process. The grain orientation randomises as revealed in the
                                                                      coloured IPF map shown in Fig. 4a.
                                                                               In contrast, the 400 nm thick CdSe layer was not fully consumed
                                                                     during activation and an approximately 300 nm thick CdSe layer
                                                                 remained intact. The CdSe residue has a purely wurtzite (hexagonal)
                                                                                 crystal structure as depicted in yellow in the phase map shown in Fig. 4b
                                                              and c. This is because the stacking faults have been removed during
                                                                             activation and the cubic component has been removed. The remaining
                                                             CdSe grains are small (average grain size of ~270 nm) in comparison to
                                                                        the alloyed CdTe grains in the samples, but substantially larger than the
                                                                          as-deposited grains in the size range of < 100 nm.

                                                                                 3.5. Device modelling

                                                            SCAPS is a 1D simulation software developed at the University of
                                                                 Gent [23]. Here it is used to model the effect of the remnant CdSe layer
                                                             on device performance. The simulated device structures are SnO2:
                                                              F/SnO2/CdSeTe/CdTe and SnO2:F/SnO2/CdSe/intermediate CdSeTe
                                                                    with very small grains/CdSeTe/CdTe for the devices without and with
                                                                        the CdSe remnant layer, respectively (Fig. 10). The electrical and optical
                                                                            properties of the materials used in the device model are tabulated in
                                                                     Table 3. These material properties have been extracted from the
                                                                      experimental results and literature [23,24]. The material properties of
                                                      FTO and SnO2 are the same in the two simulated devices. The simulated
                                                                                   light J-V and EQE measurements are shown in Fig. 11 and the device
                                                                   performance parameters are listed in Table 2. These simulated results
                                                                         are consistent with the experimental measurements shown in Fig. 2. The
                                                                       device with the CdSe remnant layer has much lower efficiency, mainly
                                                              due to a significantly reduced short circuit current. This is shown in the
                                                                         dramatically decreased EQE in the short wavelength region (Fig. 11b).
                                                                                      Fig. 12a–b shows the simulated current density across the device
                                                                             structure at the maximum power point. Due to a small band gap of 1.64
                                                                       eV, the thick 300 nm CdSe residual layer absorbs a large fraction of
                                                                   photons with wavelengths shorter than 756 nm, which generates car­
                                                                                      riers of electrons and holes. However, the defects present in the high
                                                                          density of grain boundaries in the CdSe remnant and the intermediate
                                                              CdSeTe layers (with even smaller grains) cause a severe carrier recom­
                                                                             bination.  In  the  simulated  device with  the remnant CdSe  layer
                                                                                   (Fig. 12b), the electron current in the remnant CdSe layer cancels out the
                                                                       hole current and does not contribute to the total current. The partially
                                                                              positive electron current in the remnant CdSe layer indicates the
                                                          movement of electrons is opposite to the operating electron current,
                                                               which increases the carrier recombination probability in the remnant
                                                             CdSe and intermediate CdSeTe layers and the CdSe/CdSeTe interface
                                                                           [25]. Therefore, the photo-generated carriers cannot be effectively
                                                                             collected in these layers and interface. Due to the parasitic absorption in
                                                                        the CdSe remnant and the adjacent CdSeTe layer, fewer photons can
                                                                         penetrate to the underlying CdSeTe/CdTe absorber, where the carriers
                                                                  can be efficiently collected. Thus, the combination of these effects
                                                                         dramatically reduces the short circuit current and device efficiency.
